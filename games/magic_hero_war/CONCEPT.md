# Magic Hero War

**Status: planned.** Nothing here is implemented. This is a statement of intent,
not a rule set — no rules, costs, or numbers are settled yet.

Magic Hero War is [Hero War](../hero_war/RULES.md) plus magic: the same
poker-deck-on-a-kitchen-table premise, widened until it plays like a trading
card game without anybody buying a trading card game.

## What it expands

**Spells.** Hero War gives each suit one job — heal, boost, sabotage. Magic Hero
War makes magic the centre of the game: spells are a category in their own
right, cast on your turn or in response, with effects that reach past a single
attack.

**Multiple concurrent heroes.** A player fields a party rather than one champion.
Heroes take the field alongside each other, which turns targeting, protection,
and who-swings-at-whom into decisions rather than bookkeeping.

**More than two players.** Three or more players is the default case, not an
accommodation. Threat assessment, temporary alliances, and the question of who
is winning become part of the game.

**Strategically differentiated decks.** Players bring decks with distinct
identities — aggressive, defensive, spell-heavy, hero-heavy — rather than
drawing from one shared pile.

## The deck map catalogue

The catalogue is the piece that makes the rest possible with ordinary cards. A
**deck map** assigns each of the 52 cards in a physical poker deck to a richer
card definition: a hero, a spell, a piece of equipment, with its own name and
behaviour. Two players holding identical poker decks can be playing entirely
different decks, because they are reading them through different maps.

The catalogue is the published set of those maps. Intent:

- **Even power across decks.** Maps are designed and tested to be close in
  strength, so choosing a deck is a choice of style, not of advantage.
- **Playable off the page.** A map has to be readable as a printed sheet next to
  a poker deck, so a table can play with no app at all.
- **Data, not code.** Maps are declarative — a new deck is a new entry in the
  catalogue, not a new engine.
- **Open to homebrew.** The same format anyone plays with is the format anyone
  can write.

## Props

One standard poker deck per player, the printed deck maps they are using, and
dice (d6 and d20) for effects that call for them.

## Still open

Party size and the action economy across several heroes; when spells may be cast
and what can answer them; how decks are constructed and legality is defined; and
how power parity between maps is actually measured rather than asserted.
